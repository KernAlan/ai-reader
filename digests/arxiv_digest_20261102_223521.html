Verify--Backtrack Loop: Structured Precondition Recovery for Long-Horizon Language Agents

R:9/10 I:8/10 A:8/10 8.5
Language agents increasingly tackle long-horizon tasks in interactive environments, where actions often depend on latent preconditions. The core difficulty is not trajectory length but recovery: when a prerequisite is missing or an earlier decomposition fails, trying another plausible action rarely helps. ReAct and later reflection or adaptive-planning methods support local correction, but express recovery as free-form text, with no explicit control state linking the terminal goal, the active goal, and the failure source. We introduce the Verify--Backtrack Loop (VBL), a training-free extension of ReAct that turns precondition discovery and failure diagnosis into structured planning-state updates. VBL maintains the task decomposition as a bounded goal stack and, on goal activation, execution failure, or material state change, jointly verifies two horizons---whether the active goal contributes to the terminal goal and whether it is feasible in the current state. These dual-horizon judgments, combined with typed failure attribution, trigger explicit operations---Push, Pop, retry, or BacktrackTo the parent. We evaluate VBL with Qwen3.5-9B, GPT-4o, and DeepSeek-V3.2 on AppWorld, ALFWorld, and ScienceWorld. VBL improves over ReAct and reflection-based control on every backbone--benchmark pair. On the challenging AppWorld setting, it improves task success by up to 22.07 percentage points over ReAct. Ablations show that removing either verification or diagnosis degrades performance, in several settings below ReAct, confirming that the two mechanisms are complementary. These results show that reliable long-horizon interaction benefits from revising the planning state, not merely the next action.

Context as Infrastructure: Knowledge Compilation for Retrieval in Agentic Systems with Harnesses

R:9/10 I:7/10 A:8/10 8.0
Retrieval-augmented generation is becoming agentic. A search harness writes queries, reads results, reformulates, and decides when to stop. This largely solves accuracy: a harness with only BM25/grep and MiniMax M3 answers 85% of BrowseComp-Plus and 91% on multi-hop FRAMES in our runs, without compiled or vector indexes. It exposes a context problem: an undisciplined loop repeats queries, rereads documents, and fills the window with unused passages. The missing layer is knowledge compilation: a configurable ingestion pipeline where LLMs enrich documents with context no chunk contains alone--navigation hierarchies with claim-evidence leaves, entity graphs, timelines, mind maps, ontologies, wikis, and memory. Compilation is not a better retriever: under a competent harness, structural artifacts gave no detectable accuracy gain on FRAMES, and a PageIndex-style outline degraded at scale. It is a supply layer. A navigation tree whose leaves are claim-evidence pairs--the mechanism we measure--cuts query tokens by 91% when evidence suffices and adds 1.9% when it does not, a 38% average reduction; this implies a 2% break-even sufficiency rate and a measured rate near 43%. A decision gate cuts latency from 140 s to 30 s, though substitution's token saving is bought with retrieval rounds and carries its own latency cost. Other artifacts--ontology, graph, wiki, timeline, mind map, memory--pay off in deployment (governance, integration, onboarding, temporal orientation, continuity) where accuracy benchmarks cannot see them. We describe RAGFlow's pipeline and harness (open source at https://github.com/infiniflow/ragflow), which turns the loop into five disciplined stations and keeps the context entering the model the smallest sufficient set; together they form an agent-native database: a store that hands the agent compiled, governed context instead of a ranked list of passages.

SecPO: Principled Adversarial Training for Prompt Injection Security

R:8/10 I:7/10 A:8/10 7.5
Recognized as a top threat to AI agents, prompt injection allows instructions from untrusted data (email, documents, websites) to manipulate the agent's operation. The existing best-defended LLM, Meta-SecAlign, is trained with the DPO loss but still suffers from attack success rates (ASRs) approaching 100% under adaptive prompt injections. One reason is that DPO anchors the model around the undefended reference model, which is insecure. We show an idealized reference model---an injection-invariant, utility-preserving oracle---can nevertheless be approximated, as its output distribution in training is interestingly accessible. This new observation motivates a theoretically principled SecPO loss. Another Meta-SecAlign shortcoming is that it trains on weak simulated attacks. We implement an efficient adversarial training (AT) pipeline that synthesizes strong injected inputs (best@240), and train the model on them. Our AT reduces ASRs by 1.5-14.5$\times$ across three SoTA adaptive attacks, compared with Meta-SecAlign; the SecPO loss reduces them by a further 2.4-4.6$\times$ compared with using DPO loss. In agentic workflows, our SecPO + AT Qwen3.6-27B is much more secure than Meta-SecAlign: 8.8% vs. 78.8% AgentDojo ASR@800 under the AttackerMovesSecond attack. Moreover, our defended model shows no noticeable reduction in benign utility in nine benchmarks covering agentic tool calling, instruction following, science, and math. We release our $\href{//github.com/Sizhe-Chen/SecPO}{\text{code}}$, $\href{//huggingface.co/datasets/Sizhe-Chen/Qwen3.6-27B-Instruct-SecPO-trainset}{\text{training data}}$ (with optimized injections from AT), and $\href{//huggingface.co/Sizhe-Chen/Qwen3.6-27B-Instruct-SecPO}{\text{model}}$.

Lineage Is Memory: Parent Inheritance and Feedback Disclosure in Short-Budget LLM-Guided Optimization

R:8/10 I:7/10 A:7/10 7.5
Iterative language-model optimizers refine prompts and programs by evaluating a candidate, showing a proposer model a sample of failing cases, and keeping revisions that improve performance. Recent work attributes the limits of this loop to narrow single-round reflection, introducing full-set error clustering, targeted minibatches, or larger proposer models so the optimizer sees more failure modes per step. Yet every existing evaluation tests these mechanisms exclusively inside lineage-preserving search, where each revision edits a surviving parent, leaving the contribution of parent inheritance confounded with the breadth of per-round feedback. Across 1,750 pre-registered runs spanning nine prompt and program synthesis benchmarks, we separate parent inheritance from feedback disclosure under matched evaluation budgets. We find that greedy parent inheritance drives the vast majority of optimization progress, outperforming seed-reset resampling even when seed-reset proposals receive a clustered summary of all failures in every round or use a larger proposer model. While full-set error clustering and diverse failure selection improve single-step rewrites from the seed and accelerate the opening rounds of search, sequential parent inheritance rapidly closes this gap as solved error modes are retained in the candidate and filtered from subsequent failure lists. Moreover, decomposing token expenditure in GEPA reveals that proposer reflection accounts for only a small fraction of total cost whereas validation and minibatch gating consume nearly the entire budget, showing that greedy parent inheritance over raw failures captures the gains of complex reflective loops at a fraction of their compute.

Therefore, we propose ACCT (Asymmetric Cross-LLM Context Transfer), which targets KV cache transfer between models of different scales within the same model family. ACCT consists of three lightweight components: the Cartographer identifies query-relevant context tokens for each target layer and KV head; the Allocator determines how many context tokens each target layer and KV head retains under a shared total KV budget; and the Compiler transforms selected source KV into sparse target KV. Experiments show that ACCT matches or exceeds the target model's original inference performance even when reducing the KV cache size by 75%. At a context length of approximately 19K tokens, ACCT's KV conversion achieves a 6.5-21.2 times speedup over target context prefill. These results demonstrate the feasibility of transferring context computation across models under a limited KV budget.
